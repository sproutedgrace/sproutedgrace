The Appointed Feasts

A guide for any family to keep the feasts of Leviticus 23 at home: the story, the reason, the dates, and how to host each one.

"With desire I have desired to eat this passover with you before I suffer"

LUKE 22:15

The GodFather gave His people seven holy feasts, plus the weekly Sabbath. They are found in Leviticus 23. He calls them His "appointed times." This page gives you what you need to keep them at home with your family, even if you have never done it before.

On this page

The basics · Dates for 2027 · Sabbath · Passover · Unleavened Bread · Firstfruits · Weeks · Trumpets · Day of Atonement · Tabernacles

The basics

Why keep the feasts?

  • To remember. Each feast tells part of the story of how the GodFather freed and fed His people.
  • To see Jesus. He kept these feasts, and many of them point to Him.
  • To rejoice. They are times to rest, eat and celebrate together.
  • To teach our children. A feast you can taste, see and touch is a lesson they keep for life.

Three things to know first

  • A Bible day starts at sunset. Each feast begins the evening before the date, and ends at sunset (Leviticus 23:32).
  • The months follow the moon. That is why the dates move each year on our calendar.
  • The year starts in spring. The first month, Aviv (also called Nisan), comes in March or April (Exodus 12:2).

Rest days

Some feast days are "holy convocations." On those days, the GodFather asked His people to rest from their usual work. Each feast below tells you which days those are.

By conscience

No one is required to keep the feasts. Start small. Keep one this year, and add more as your heart leads.

"For they bind heavy burdens and grievous to be borne, and lay them on men's shoulders"

MATTHEW 23:4

Every week

The Sabbath

When: Friday at sunset to Saturday at sunset.

The Sabbath is the first appointed time in Leviticus 23 (verse 3). It is a weekly day of rest. A simple Friday night meal is a beautiful way to begin it:

  1. Before sunset, finish your work and set the table.
  2. Light two candles. Thank the GodFather for the week and for rest.
  3. Bless a cup of wine or grape juice, and share it.
  4. Bless a loaf of bread, break it, and share it.
  5. Speak a blessing over each child or each person at the table.
  6. Eat slowly. Talk, laugh and pray.

Spring · 1st month, 14th day

Passover (Pesach)

When: The evening of the 14th day of the first month. In 2027, the evening of April 21.

The story

The people of Israel were slaves in Egypt. The GodFather sent ten plagues. Before the last one, each family killed a lamb and put its blood on their doorposts. That night, death passed over every house marked by the blood. Pharaoh let the people go. They left in such a hurry that their bread had no time to rise (Exodus 12).

Leviticus 23:5 · Exodus 12

Jesus and this feast

Jesus' Last Supper was a Passover meal. He took the bread and the cup of Passover and gave them new meaning. Communion comes from this meal.

"This is my body which is given for you: this do in remembrance of me"

LUKE 22:19

How to host it

What you need

  • Unleavened bread (matzah from the store, or homemade: see Unleavened Bread below)
  • Wine or grape juice, with a cup for each person
  • Bitter herbs: horseradish or romaine lettuce
  • A small bowl of salt water
  • Charoset: chopped apples, nuts, honey and cinnamon, mixed
  • Roasted lamb, or another meat or main dish
  • A Bible, open to Exodus 12 and Luke 22
  1. Begin. At sunset, light candles and pray. Thank the GodFather for freedom.
  2. First cup. Lift your cups and give thanks. Drink.
  3. Tell the story. Read Exodus 12, or tell it in your own words. Let a child ask: "What mean ye by this service?" (Exodus 12:26).
  4. Bitter herbs. Dip them in salt water and eat. They stand for the bitterness of slavery and the tears it caused.
  5. Charoset. Eat it on a piece of bread. It looks like the mortar slaves used to make bricks, but it tastes sweet, like freedom.
  6. The bread. Break the unleavened bread. Read Luke 22:19, and share it.
  7. The meal. Enjoy dinner together.
  8. The cup after supper. Read Luke 22:20. Share the cup.
  9. Close. Sing a hymn, as Jesus did (Matthew 26:30), and pray.

For children

  • Let them hunt for a piece of hidden bread after dinner, with a small prize for the finder.
  • Ask them to act out the plagues or the doorposts.
  • Give them the job of asking the questions in the story.
Spring · 7 days after Passover

Unleavened Bread

When: The 15th to the 21st day of the first month. In 2027, April 22 to April 29. Rest on the first and last days.

The story

Because Israel left Egypt in a hurry, they ate bread without yeast. For seven days, the GodFather asked His people to eat no leavened bread and to remove all yeast from their homes. In Scripture, leaven often stands for sin and pride, which spread quietly through a whole life.

Leviticus 23:6-8 · Exodus 12:15-20

Jesus and this feast

"Take heed and beware of the leaven of the Pharisees and of the Sadducees"

MATTHEW 16:6

How to host it

What you need

  • Unleavened bread for the week
  • A flashlight and a small brush (for the leaven hunt)
  • A simple flatbread recipe (below)
  1. Before the feast, clean out bread, crackers and anything made with yeast. Give it away or use it up.
  2. Leaven hunt. On the night before, search the house with a flashlight for any last crumbs. Sweep them up and throw them out.
  3. All week, eat only unleavened bread.
  4. Rest from work on the first and last days.
  5. Each day, pray about one "leaven" in your own life to remove: pride, a grudge, a habit.

For children

  • Hide a few crumbs for them to find on the leaven hunt.
  • Let them help bake the flatbread.

Simple unleavened flatbread

  • 2 cups flour, 2/3 cup water, 1/3 cup olive oil, 1 teaspoon salt.
  • Mix, roll thin, and poke with a fork.
  • Bake at 450°F for 8 to 10 minutes, until crisp.
Spring · During Unleavened Bread

Firstfruits

When: The Sunday during the week of Unleavened Bread. In 2027, April 25.

The story

When the barley harvest began, the people brought the first sheaf to the priest. He waved it before the GodFather. Only after that could they eat from the new harvest. The first and best was given back to Him.

Leviticus 23:9-14

Jesus and this feast

Jesus rose from the dead on this day, the first day of the week during Unleavened Bread. He is the first of the great harvest of all who will rise.

"Except a corn of wheat fall into the ground and die, it abideth alone: but if it die, it bringeth forth much fruit"

JOHN 12:24

How to host it

What you need

  • A sheaf of grain, a bundle of wheat stalks, or any first fruit of your garden or work
  • A sunrise spot outdoors, if you can
  1. Rise early and gather at sunrise, as the women did at the tomb.
  2. Read the resurrection story (Luke 24:1-12).
  3. Give your first. Set aside the first part of something new, like the first pay of the week or the first of your garden, for the GodFather or for someone in need.
  4. Share a breakfast together and celebrate.

For children

  • Plant a seed together and talk about John 12:24.
  • Let them hold or wave the bundle of grain.
Late spring · 50 days after Firstfruits

Weeks (Shavuot, Pentecost)

When: Count 50 days from Firstfruits. In 2027, that is Sunday, June 13. (The Jewish calendar counts from the day after Passover and keeps it June 11.) Rest on this day.

The story

After the barley came the wheat harvest. The GodFather told His people to count seven weeks, then hold a feast with two loaves of bread made from the new wheat. Jewish tradition also remembers this as the time the law was given at Mount Sinai. And on this very feast, the Holy Spirit came upon the disciples (Acts 2).

Leviticus 23:15-22 · Acts 2

Jesus and this feast

Before He left, Jesus promised the Spirit would come. It came on this feast.

"But the Comforter, which is the Holy Ghost, whom the Father will send in my name, he shall teach you all things"

JOHN 14:26

How to host it

What you need

  • A paper chain or calendar to count the 49 days
  • Ingredients for two loaves of bread
  • Food to give to someone in need
  1. Count the days. Starting at Firstfruits, mark off one day each evening for 49 days.
  2. Bake two loaves of bread together the day before.
  3. Read the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20) and the coming of the Spirit (Acts 2).
  4. Share a meal. Many families serve dairy foods and fresh bread.
  5. Remember the poor. The GodFather told farmers to leave the edges of their fields for the poor (Leviticus 23:22). Give food or a meal to someone in need.
  6. Pray for the Holy Spirit to fill each person.

For children

  • Make a paper chain of 49 links, and tear one off each night.
  • Let them help knead the two loaves.
Fall · 7th month, 1st day

Trumpets (Yom Teruah)

When: The first day of the seventh month. In 2027, the evening of October 1 to October 3. Rest on this day.

The story

On this day, trumpets and rams' horns (shofars) were blown across the land. It was a loud call to wake up, remember the GodFather, and get ready for the holy days ahead. Jewish tradition keeps it as the start of the new year.

Leviticus 23:23-25 · Numbers 29:1

Jesus and this feast

"And he shall send his angels with a great sound of a trumpet, and they shall gather together his elect"

MATTHEW 24:31

How to host it

What you need

  • A shofar, trumpet, or any horn (even a toy one)
  • Apples and honey
  • Paper and pens
  1. Blow the horn at sunset. Let everyone take a turn.
  2. Read Leviticus 23:23-25 and Matthew 24:31.
  3. Dip apples in honey and pray for a sweet year ahead.
  4. Wake up. Each person writes down one thing to set right before the Day of Atonement, ten days away.

For children

  • Let them blow the horn.
  • Make a short list together of things the family is thankful for.
Fall · 7th month, 10th day

Day of Atonement (Yom Kippur)

When: The tenth day of the seventh month. In 2027, the evening of October 10 to October 11. A day of rest and fasting.

The story

Once a year, the high priest entered the holiest place in the tabernacle to make atonement for the sins of the people. The GodFather told everyone to "afflict your souls": to fast, rest and humbly turn back to Him. It is the most solemn day of the year.

Leviticus 23:26-32 · Leviticus 16

Jesus and this feast

Jesus gave His own life so we could be made right with the GodFather.

"I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep"

JOHN 10:11

How to host it

What you need

  • A quiet place
  • Paper and pen for reflection
  • A simple meal to break the fast
  1. Eat a good meal before sunset, then begin the fast.
  2. Fast from food from sunset to sunset, if you are able. Drink water.
  3. Reflect. Look honestly at your heart. The seven gates can help: fear, guilt, anger, hurt, frustration, disappointment and inadequacy. Where are you stuck?
  4. Make it right. Ask forgiveness from anyone you have wronged. Forgive those who wronged you.
  5. Pray and rest through the day.
  6. Break the fast at sunset with a simple meal and thanks.

For children

  • Children should not fast. They can give up one treat instead.
  • Help them write or draw one thing they are sorry for, and one thing they forgive.

A word on fasting

Do not fast if you are pregnant, nursing, diabetic, ill, elderly, or take medicine that must be taken with food. Eat simply instead. The heart matters more than the fast. Ask your doctor if you are unsure.

Fall · 7th month, 15th day, for 7 days

Tabernacles (Sukkot)

When: The 15th to the 21st day of the seventh month, then an eighth day of rest. In 2027, the evening of October 15 to October 22, then October 23. Rest on the first and eighth days.

The story

For forty years in the wilderness, Israel lived in tents, and the GodFather fed and guided them. To remember, He told His people to live for seven days in booths, called sukkahs, made with leafy branches. It is the most joyful feast of the year, a harvest party under the open sky.

Leviticus 23:33-43 · Deuteronomy 16:13-15

Jesus and this feast

Jesus went up to Jerusalem for this feast. On its last and greatest day, He stood and cried out:

"If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink"

JOHN 7:37

How to host it

What you need

  • Poles, boards or PVC pipe for a frame (or a tent or camper)
  • Leafy branches, palm fronds or corn stalks for the roof
  • Fruit, lights and decorations
  • A table and chairs that fit inside
  1. Build a booth before the feast. Make three walls and a roof of branches loose enough to see the stars through.
  2. Decorate it with fruit, lights and the children's art.
  3. Eat your meals in the booth all week. Sleep in it if you can. Camping works too.
  4. Invite guests. This is a feast of welcome. Ask friends, neighbors or someone who is alone.
  5. Read Leviticus 23:33-43 and John 7:37-39.
  6. Rejoice. Sing, play music and give thanks for the harvest.
  7. Rest on the first day and the eighth day.

For children

  • Let them help build and decorate the booth.
  • Count the stars through the roof and talk about the GodFather's promises.
  • Tell the story of the manna and the wilderness at bedtime.

Getting started

  1. Pick one feast to keep this year. Passover is a great first one.
  2. Mark the date on your calendar, and gather what you need a week early.
  3. Read this page through once with your family.
  4. Keep it simple. A humble feast kept with love is better than a perfect one.
  5. Join us online, and share how it went.
