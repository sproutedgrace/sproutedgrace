Plant Medicine

We believe the GodFather gave plants for healing. Received with reverence, they can be holy communion with the Spirit.

What we believe

  • The body was made on purpose. That includes the endocannabinoid system, which helps the body keep its balance.
  • Plants were given for healing. The leaves of the tree of life are "for the healing of the nations" (Revelation 22:2).
  • The holy anointing oil. We hold that kaneh bosem, an ingredient of the anointing oil in Exodus 30:23, was cannabis. We will make this oil only once we are legally exempt.
  • A sober mind. To be sober-minded is to keep a clear, watchful mind under the GodFather.

"Take heed to yourselves, lest at any time your hearts be overcharged with surfeiting, and drunkenness"

LUKE 21:34

How the sacrament is kept

  • It is for prayer and healing only. It is never sold.
  • Cannabis is given only by a qualified ordained minister.
  • Other plant medicines are given only by a trained medicine man or woman approved by the ministry.
  • Only adults take part. Each one is screened and agrees in writing.
  • At least one person who has not taken part keeps watch.
  • Every ceremony is recorded.
  • It is always a free choice. No member is ever required to take part.

Walking with the law

We follow the law in this. The ministry plans to seek a religious exemption from the DEA, under the Religious Freedom Restoration Act. Until then, no controlled substance is used in our ceremonies unless that use is lawful where it is held.

Some medicines do not mix with plant medicines. Talk with your doctor about any medicines you take.

Retreats

We are planning retreats of prayer, rest and healing, led by trained medicine men and women. Our first retreats will center on ayahuasca and ibogaine. Retreats with psilocybin and MDMA are planned for later.

Each retreat will be held only where that medicine is lawful, or after the ministry receives its exemption. The sacrament itself is never sold.

Why these medicines

Many people come to these medicines after years of addiction, trauma or depression that nothing else touched. Many say they found deep healing, sometimes in a single ceremony, and some say one night changed the rest of their lives.

Early research is hopeful. In a 2024 Stanford study, 30 military veterans with brain injuries received one ibogaine treatment. One month later, their symptoms of PTSD, depression and anxiety had dropped sharply. Studies of psilocybin and MDMA have shown promise too.

The opposite of addiction is connection

In the "Rat Park" studies of the 1970s, Dr. Bruce Alexander found that rats kept alone in cages drank far more drugged water than rats living in a rich, shared community. Writer Johann Hari put the lesson simply: "the opposite of addiction is connection."

That is the heart of our retreats. The medicine opens a door. Prayer, community and the GodFather's love help a person walk through it, and keep walking long after the retreat ends.

Honest about the risks

These are powerful medicines, and they deserve respect. Results are different for each person, and no one can promise a cure.

  • Ibogaine can cause dangerous heart rhythms and has been linked to deaths. It requires a heart test, medical screening and medical watch during treatment.
  • Ayahuasca can be dangerous with antidepressants and some other medicines.
  • Anyone with heart problems, a history of psychosis, or who takes certain medicines may not be able to take part.

That is why every guest is screened, prepared and watched over, and why we walk with them afterward.
